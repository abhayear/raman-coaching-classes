## Worked example with answer

**Example (try first):**

Compare reaction rate: CH₃Br vs (CH₃)₃CBr with I⁻ in acetone.

**Real-life link:** Acrylic acid in superabsorbent nappies polymerizes through conjugate systems; Michael addition installs side chains in many polymer recipes.

---

**Answer:**

CH₃Br reacts **much faster** (SN2). Methyl/primary halides have minimal steric hindrance; tert-butyl blocks backside approach. (CH₃)₃CBr favours SN1/E1, not SN2.

## Practice problems with answers

Try each problem first, then check the answer shown directly below.

**Problem 1 (pKa of conjugate acid):** Is CH₃OH a good nucleophile in acid or base?

**Answer:**

In **base**: CH₃O⁻ is strong nucleophile. In **acid**: mostly CH₃OH (neutral), poor nucleophile; protonation of alcohol gives R–OH₂⁺ (leaving group in substitution).

---

**Problem 2 (Polarizability):** Primary vs tertiary alkyl halide + NaI in acetone — which reacts faster?

**Answer:**

**Primary/methyl faster** (SN2). Tertiary blocked sterically; may go SN1 if polar protic solvent used.
