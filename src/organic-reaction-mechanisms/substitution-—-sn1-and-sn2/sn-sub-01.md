## Worked example with answer

**Example (try first):**

(R)-2-bromobutane is treated with NaCN in acetone. Predict the stereochemistry of the product and name the mechanism.

**Real-life link:** Soap removes grease because the carboxylate head (nucleophile) attacks ester bonds in cooking fats — the same electron-donation logic drives SN2 in the lab.

---

**Answer:**

SN2 — backside attack inverts configuration. (R)-2-bromobutane → **(S)-2-methylbutanenitrile**. Rate = k[substrate][CN⁻]; polar aprotic acetone stabilizes CN⁻ without strong solvation shell.

## Practice problems with answers

Try each problem first, then check the answer shown directly below.

**Problem 1 (Nucleophilicity):** Is CH₃OH a good nucleophile in acid or base?

**Answer:**

In **base**: CH₃O⁻ is strong nucleophile. In **acid**: mostly CH₃OH (neutral), poor nucleophile; protonation of alcohol gives R–OH₂⁺ (leaving group in substitution).

---

**Problem 2 (Electrophilicity):** Primary vs tertiary alkyl halide + NaI in acetone — which reacts faster?

**Answer:**

**Primary/methyl faster** (SN2). Tertiary blocked sterically; may go SN1 if polar protic solvent used.

---

**Problem 3 (Solvent effects):** Primary vs tertiary alkyl halide + NaI in acetone — which reacts faster?

**Answer:**
