## Worked example with answer

**Example (try first):**

(R)-2-bromobutane is treated with NaCN in acetone. Predict the stereochemistry of the product and name the mechanism.

**Real-life link:** Mirror-image limonene smells like orange (R) vs pine (S) — stereochemistry is not abstract; it's why enantiopure drugs cost more to manufacture.

---

**Answer:**

SN2 — backside attack inverts configuration. (R)-2-bromobutane → **(S)-2-methylbutanenitrile**. Rate = k[substrate][CN⁻]; polar aprotic acetone stabilizes CN⁻ without strong solvation shell.
