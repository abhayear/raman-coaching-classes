## Worked example with answer

**Example (try first):**

Is CH₃OH a good nucleophile in acid or base?

**Real-life link:** Free-radical chain reactions age your skin (UV + O2), cure dental composites (peroxide initiators), and bleach hair (H2O2 + melanin radicals).

---

**Answer:**

In **base**: CH₃O⁻ is strong nucleophile. In **acid**: mostly CH₃OH (neutral), poor nucleophile; protonation of alcohol gives R–OH₂⁺ (leaving group in substitution).

## Practice problems with answers

Try each problem first, then check the answer shown directly below.

**Problem 1 (Homolysis):** Is CH₃OH a good nucleophile in acid or base?

**Answer:**

In **base**: CH₃O⁻ is strong nucleophile. In **acid**: mostly CH₃OH (neutral), poor nucleophile; protonation of alcohol gives R–OH₂⁺ (leaving group in substitution).

---

**Problem 2 (Chain kinetics):** First-order reaction: t½ = 10 min. Fraction remaining after 30 min?

**Answer:**

3 half-lives → (1/2)³ = **1/8 = 12.5%** remaining. Real life: aspirin hydrolysis shelf-life uses identical math.

---

**Problem 3 (Homolysis):** Primary vs tertiary alkyl halide + NaI in acetone — which reacts faster?

**Answer:**

**Primary/methyl faster** (SN2). Tertiary blocked sterically; may go SN1 if polar protic solvent used.
