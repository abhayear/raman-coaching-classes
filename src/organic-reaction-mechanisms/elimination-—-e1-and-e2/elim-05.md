## Core concepts (each linked to real life)

### 1. Axial leaving groups

**Real-life connection:** A bad leaving group is like a weak handshake — the bond won't break. Iodide leaves
easily (weak bond); fluoride rarely leaves. That's why HI promotes substitution better
than HF.

## Practice problems with answers

Try each problem first, then check the answer shown directly below.

**Problem 1 (Axial leaving groups):** Is CH₃OH a good nucleophile in acid or base?

**Answer:**

In **base**: CH₃O⁻ is strong nucleophile. In **acid**: mostly CH₃OH (neutral), poor nucleophile; protonation of alcohol gives R–OH₂⁺ (leaving group in substitution).

---

**Problem 2 (Conformation):** Primary vs tertiary alkyl halide + NaI in acetone — which reacts faster?

**Answer:**

**Primary/methyl faster** (SN2). Tertiary blocked sterically; may go SN1 if polar protic solvent used.
